FILE 00The short version

Dark Grid is a solo-built zombie survival game. It doesn’t ask who you are, it doesn’t show ads, and it doesn’t follow you around other apps or websites.

The only data that leaves your phone is anonymous gameplay and performance data sent to Google Firebase, plus an optional copy of your save in your own iCloud or Google account. Everything below is the long version, so you can check my work.

FILE 01What’s collected

Dark Grid doesn’t collect personal information. It uses two Google Firebase services: Analytics (how the game is played and how it performs) and Cloud Messaging (optional notifications). Here is exactly what that covers.

Data that leaves your device
WhatExamplesWhyWho gets it
Gameplay and progress A round’s difficulty, survivor, weapon, level, kills, score and length; what you buy with in-game watts; contracts, medals and rank-ups; which screens you open; which settings you change. Individual kills and taps are never logged. To balance the game and see which features get used. Google Firebase Analytics
Performance and errors Average and lowest frame rate, how often the game stutters, how long it took to start, and when it hits an unexpected error: the type of error, the opening of its message, and the screen you were on. To find phones the game runs badly on, and fix bugs. Google Firebase Analytics
Device and install Device model, OS version, app version, language, approximate region, a random install ID, and whether the build came from a store or is a test build. To split reports by device, and keep my own test builds out of the numbers. Google Firebase Analytics
Notification token (only if you allow notifications) A push token for your device, subscribed to two broad topics: “all” and your platform. No personal details. To deliver contract, streak and news notifications. Google Firebase Cloud Messaging, and Apple’s push service on iPhone and iPad
Cloud save (only if you’re signed in) Your progress file: watts, the grid, upgrades, contracts, medals, streak and settings. To keep your progress across your own devices. Your own iCloud or Google Play Games account. Not me.

Where the device information comes from

Google Analytics collects the device model, OS and app version, language and country or city-level region automatically. The region is worked out from your IP address; Google doesn’t log or store the IP address itself in Analytics. The install ID is a random number Firebase makes for each installation. It isn’t linked to your name, your email or your Apple or Google account, and a fresh one is created if you reinstall.

Notifications are optional

The game asks about notifications once, after your first round ends, never at launch and never during a round. Say no and everything works exactly the same. You can change your mind any time in your system settings, or from Settings inside the game. Notifications never interrupt a round in progress.

About advertising identifiers

There are no ads in Dark Grid. On iPhone and iPad the game never shows the App Tracking Transparency prompt, so iOS doesn’t hand it your advertising identifier. On Android, Google’s analytics library may read the Android advertising ID, where your device provides one, only to count users and installs, never to show ads. You can reset or delete it in your Android settings (Privacy → Ads).

What I never collect

Dark Grid doesn’t ask for, and has no way to read:

FILE 02Your game progress

Your watts, the lit grid, upgrades, contracts, medals, streak, stats and settings are saved in a file inside the game’s private storage on your device. That file is never uploaded to me. Delete the game and it goes with it.

Cloud save

Where your device offers it, the game also keeps a copy of that file in your own cloud account: iCloud key-value storage on iPhone and iPad, Google Play Games Saved Games on Android. That is how your progress follows you to a new phone or a second device. The copy lives under your Apple or Google account and their privacy policies. I can’t see it, read it or reach it.

If you’re not signed in to iCloud or Play Games, the game simply carries on with the local save.

FILE 03How the data is used

Strictly to make the game better and to keep it running:

  • Balancing difficulty and pacing, such as how long rounds last and where players tend to fall.
  • Finding phones and OS versions where the game stutters or fails to start, and fixing them.
  • Deciding which features are worth building next.
  • Sending the optional notifications you’ve allowed.

Never

I don’t sell data. I don’t share it with advertisers or data brokers, build advertising profiles, track you across other companies’ apps or websites, or try to work out who any individual player is.

FILE 04Third-party services

Dark Grid is built on a short list of services, and these are all of them. Each handles data under its own privacy policy:

  • Google Firebase (Analytics and Cloud Messaging), for the data in File 01. Firebase privacy information
  • Apple iCloud and the Apple Push Notification service, for cloud save and for delivering notifications on iPhone and iPad. Apple’s privacy policy
  • Google Play Games Services and Google Play, for cloud save on Android. Google’s privacy policy

There are no ad networks, no social networks, no payment processors and no other analytics or crash-reporting services in the game. The App Store and Google Play can show me aggregate download and crash figures from players who’ve opted in to share them. That comes from the store, not from the game.

FILE 05Data linked to you

Dark Grid has no accounts, so nothing it collects is connected to your name, email, phone number or any other identity. The analytics and notification data hangs off a random install ID, and that’s all.

FILE 06Data retention

  • Analytics data: kept by Google for at most 14 months, the longest Google Analytics allows, then deleted automatically.
  • Install ID and push token: until you uninstall the game. A reinstall starts with a new random ID.
  • Save file on your device: until you delete the game.
  • Cloud save: until you remove it from your iCloud or Google account. It’s your copy, so it stays until you say otherwise.

FILE 07Children’s privacy

Dark Grid is a zombie game with cartoon-style violence and isn’t directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, and I don’t knowingly collect personal information from children. If you’re a parent or guardian and think something has gone wrong, please get in touch (see File 11).

FILE 08Your rights and choices

  • Notifications: switch them off any time in your device’s system settings.
  • Cloud save: sign out of iCloud (iPhone and iPad) or Play Games (Android), and the game keeps going from the local save alone.
  • Everything else: deleting the game stops all collection and discards its install ID.

Depending on where you live (the EU and UK under GDPR, California under the CCPA and CPRA, and similar laws elsewhere) you may have the right to access, correct, delete or restrict data about you, or to object to how it’s used. Because Dark Grid has no accounts, I can’t tell which analytics records belong to you, which means I can’t look them up or edit them one by one. They’re pseudonymous and expire on their own. If you’d like to exercise a right or just ask a question, get in touch and I’ll do everything I reasonably can.

FILE 09Security

Anything the game sends goes to Google over encrypted connections (HTTPS/TLS). Your save sits in the game’s private storage on your device, and in your own cloud account if sync is on. No system is perfectly secure, but the best protection here is also the simplest: there’s very little to protect, and nothing in it that identifies you.

FILE 10Changes to this policy

If anything here changes, such as a new service, accounts, ads, an online leaderboard or purchases, I’ll update this page before the change ships and move the “last updated” date at the top. Meaningful changes will also be called out in the game’s release notes.
